Gaming laptop or ultrabook

Both are laptops, yet they are built for different jobs. Here is how they compare side by side.

Gaming laptops

Maximum graphics power, fast screens and strong cooling.

  • Dedicated NVIDIA RTX or AMD Radeon graphics
  • 144 to 240 Hz displays
  • Vapour chamber and dual fan cooling
  • Typically 2.0 to 3.2 kg

Best for gamers, 3D artists and engineers.

Ultrabooks

Slim, light and built to last all day on one charge.

  • 10 to 20 hours of battery life
  • Slim aluminium bodies under 17 mm
  • Sharp 2.8K and OLED screen options
  • Typically 0.9 to 1.5 kg

Best for business users, students and developers.

Technical deep dive

Explore how the two types differ under the surface.

Understanding the trade-off

Smaller laptop bodies have limited surface area to shed heat, so engineers must trade power against portability.

Gaming laptops spend thickness on cooling so they can sustain a high power draw. Ultrabooks save energy with low power processors and integrated graphics, which lets the body stay slim.

How each type rates

Gaming laptops

A dedicated graphics chip with its own video memory does the heavy lifting. Total graphics power can reach 140 to 175 W on top configurations.

  • Cooling. Vapour chamber or several heat pipes with fast fans.
  • Noise. Roughly 45 to 52 dB under full load.
  • Sustained speed. Holds high clock speeds for long sessions, though poorly cooled models can still throttle.

Ultrabooks

Low voltage processors in the 15 to 28 W range share integrated graphics such as Intel Arc, AMD Radeon or Apple unified memory.

  • Cooling. A single quiet fan or a fanless heat spreader.
  • Noise. Often below 30 dB, or completely silent.
  • Sustained speed. Quick in short bursts, but slows down during long 3D renders.

Refresh rate

165 to 240 Hz

Gaming laptops chase fast refresh and response times of 1 to 3 ms, which reduces motion blur and input lag.

Resolution and contrast

2.8K to 4K

Ultrabooks favour dense, high contrast OLED or Mini-LED panels for crisp text and photo editing.

Build materials

Metal or mixed

Ultrabooks use CNC aluminium or carbon fibre for a thin, rigid body. Gaming laptops often mix reinforced plastic and metal to make room for cooling.

Gaming laptops

3 to 6 hours

Typical light use. Full graphics load drains the battery in well under two hours, and the charger can add around 0.8 kg to your bag.

Ultrabooks

10 to 20 hours

Comfortably covers a working day unplugged, and recharges from a compact 65 W USB-C charger.

Buying guide

Model names hide large differences. These checks apply to either type and help you avoid costly mistakes.

Before a gaming laptop

  • Find the graphics power limit for the exact model, since it shapes real performance more than the GPU name.
  • Read reviews that report temperatures and fan noise during long sessions.
  • Weigh the charger as well as the laptop, because both travel with you.

Before an ultrabook

  • Buy at least 16 GB of memory if it is soldered, since it cannot be added later.
  • Look for measured battery tests instead of the manufacturer claim.
  • Check which ports are missing and budget for a dongle if you need HDMI or Ethernet.
